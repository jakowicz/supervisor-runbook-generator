## Objective

Create `projects/<project-slug>/planning/runbook-authoring-manifest.json` and
its readable Markdown companion. It is a persistent allocation ledger, not a
one-shot list: partition the currently available `IMP-*` catalogue into ordered,
small authoring batches and retain queued domain-expansion work. Each batch
will later be a normal Supervisor runbook that writes a bounded set of final
implementation runbooks.

Use IDs `B0001`, `B0002`, … for authoring batches and reserve IDs `R0001`,
`R0002`, … for generated implementation runbooks. Limit a batch to at most 7
R-series contracts and reduce that limit when context, coupling, or a platform
domain requires it. This is the scaling mechanism: a thousand implementation
runbooks become many independent, resumable authoring tasks.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every available `IMP-*` record belongs to exactly one batch or has a
  documented human gate/deferment; queued requirements identify their future
  expansion chapter. Every allocated `R-*` ID is unique and immutable.
- The manifest distinguishes **authoring order** from **implementation order**.
  B-series authors may write every allocated R-series contract as soon as its
  canonical specification and manifest record exist, including contracts with
  an unresolved human gate or an unimplemented R-series dependency. Those
  gates and dependencies belong inside the R contract and control later
  product-work execution; they must never block creation of the runbook itself.
  Batch ordering identifies only genuine authoring-context prerequisites and
  batches safe to author in parallel.
- Each batch has a context packet listing only the relevant specification,
  catalogue records, terminology, target constraints, required templates, and
  explicit provenance values for every allocated R contract: canonical
  `source_specifications`, `source_catalogue_ids`, originating
  `authoring_batch`, and the relevant `factory_stages`.
- No authoring batch is assigned more than seven R-series contracts; a smaller
  batch is required when its contracts are tightly coupled or context-heavy.
- The manifest records expected runbook count, IDs, dependencies, output paths,
  acceptance checks, checkpoint cadence, recovery instructions, and an explicit
  asset assessment for every allocated R contract. The assessment records
  `asset_impact` as either `required` or `not_applicable`; asset-required
  contracts reserve stable, product-specific `asset_ids` and identify their
  asset brief/purpose before a B-series writer is assigned.
- The manifest records an explicit audio assessment for every allocated R
  contract. It uses `audio_impact` (`required` or `not_applicable`); audio
  contracts reserve stable, product-specific `audio_ids`, a cue brief, duration,
  loop policy, and audio-style version before a B-series writer is assigned.
- When canonical asset or audio direction exists, the manifest records a
  coverage map from every required asset family and cue-map entry to at least
  one current or queued `IMP-*` record. Each allocated asset/audio contract has
  `asset_impact: required` and/or `audio_impact: required` with stable IDs; a
  foundation-only wave may defer those contracts to a named later shard but may
  not silently treat the whole product as asset-free or silent.
- A declared asset family or audio cue cannot be covered solely by a generic
  schema, provenance, or documentation contract. Its eventual allocated R
  contract must declare `asset_impact: required` and/or `audio_impact:
  required`, reserve the corresponding stable IDs, cite the canonical direction
  file, and own generation/integration or a distinct explicit QA handoff.
  The manifest records which of those roles each contract owns.
- When a narrative/content model exists, the manifest preserves its
  `narrative_coverage` map. Every first-release content family has a current or
  named queued contract for original creation, provenance/validation, runtime
  integration, and QA; a generic state, schema, or documentation contract is
  not enough to claim the player-facing story/content is covered.
- Batches preserve end-to-end vertical slices as well as foundation work, so a
  large product can be implemented and reviewed incrementally.
- A manifest `blocked_on_gate` state means the associated **R implementation**
  remains gated, not that its B-series authoring task may stop without writing
  its assigned R files. The B task must write the contract, retain the gate in
  its objective/acceptance criteria, validate it, and record that later
  implementation is blocked.
- The ledger contains monotonic next-ID counters and wave state, so a resumed
  dispatcher never renumbers accepted B-series or R-series work.
