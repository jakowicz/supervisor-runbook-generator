## Objective

Create `projects/<project-slug>/specification/04-experience-contract.md` covering original
information architecture, visual direction, interaction principles, content
style, responsive behaviour, and accessibility policy. Adapt interaction and
layout to every selected surface: touch, keyboard/mouse, remote/focus, gamepad,
voice, or assistive input as applicable.

Specify the experience appropriate to the discovered product domains, including
content tone and narrative presentation for story-led products, operational and
collaboration flows for work products, and safety/trust interactions where
relevant. A reference establishes desired capability or audience outcome only;
the resulting visual language, characters, world, writing, content, and
interaction choreography must be original.

When the product needs visual assets beyond ordinary text/layout, also create
`projects/<project-slug>/specification/04-asset-direction.md`. Define the
original visual identity; asset families and priorities; representative
locations/screens; variants and states; original-art/provenance policy;
accessibility constraints; and a scalable asset-ID convention. For example, a
game may need environment tiles, characters, portraits, UI icons, items,
effects, maps, and marketing/store imagery; another product may instead need
illustrations, diagrams, icons, templates, or document visuals.

When the product needs sound, music, voice, or audible feedback, also create
`projects/<project-slug>/specification/04-audio-direction.md`. Define the
original audio identity, a cue map (for example menu, exploration, battle,
success/failure, notifications, and accessibility feedback where relevant),
instrumental/vocal policy, loop/duration targets, mixing and accessibility
requirements, and how audio supports each product surface. Do not force asset
or audio plans onto products where they have no material user value. A game is
presumed to need both plans unless `INITIAL.md` explicitly says it is silent,
text-only, or otherwise asset-free.

## Acceptance criteria

- Navigation, empty/error states, feedback, destructive actions, and privacy-sensitive content are specified.
- Tokens, keyboard behaviour, focus, screen-reader labels, contrast, touch targets, and reduced motion are defined.
- The proposed experience is original and does not reproduce a reference product's trade dress.
- Where visual assets are in scope, the asset plan assigns stable,
  product-specific asset-family IDs, purpose, variants, provenance/originality,
  accessibility, and acceptance requirements.
- Where audio is in scope, the cue map assigns stable, product-specific cue IDs
  and purpose, loop/duration, interaction trigger, and acceptance requirements.
