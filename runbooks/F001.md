## Objective

Read the initial project brief supplied in the Supervisor context. It already
lives in `projects/<project-slug>/INITIAL.md`; use that existing workspace and
do not create a second slugged project. Copy the normalized brief to that
workspace's `PROJECT_BRIEF.md` and create `specification/01-product-brief.md`
alongside it. Define the product
problem, category, selected target systems, users, constraints, success measures,
non-goals, assumptions, and open decisions. A named app may be a functional
reference only; it is never permission to copy protected expression.

Create `specification/01-creative-direction.md` at the same time. It is the
early creative north star, not a late art-treatment document. Record its
original tone, audience feeling, world/setting or content voice where relevant,
and the initial visual and audio intent. **Every game** must establish its
narrative/gameplay framing: player fantasy and role, setting or play space,
central premise/conflict or play goal, emotional/engagement arc, first-release
content boundary, and the kinds of characters, locations, challenges, scenes,
progression, and endings or completion states it needs. This applies equally to
story RPGs, strategy games, sports games, racers, platformers, and puzzles; a
lightweight game may have lightweight narrative framing, but never none. For
non-game products, explicitly record the appropriate content/communication
direction instead. This document is an input to feature modelling, technical
content schemas, art, audio, and implementation planning.

For a game, also create `specification/01-game-identity.md`. Define the game
pillars, player fantasy, core loop, moment-to-moment verbs, session shape,
progression promise, emotional/engagement arc, audience, original creative
boundaries, and selected genre signals. Use stable `GAME-*` IDs for each
first-release pillar or design unit. This is the common game layer: it applies
to an RPG, builder, strategy game, racing game, sports game, puzzle, or
platformer without assuming that they share the same characters or story.

For a game, make the classification decision explicit before choosing any
content systems. Add a `game-design-signals` section to `01-game-identity.md`
and `00-domain-discovery.md`: game archetype(s), primary player activity,
session model, knowledge/content source, competitive/cooperative model,
creative-content intensity, and the evidence in the brief for each signal.
Use this to propose candidate design modules and explicitly reject irrelevant
ones. A football trivia game might signal question-bank provenance, categories,
difficulty calibration, answer adjudication, scoring/streak rules, modes,
anti-repetition, and editorial refresh; it does **not** acquire an RPG world,
party, bosses, or quest system merely because those are common game examples.
Conversely, an RPG can signal world, named party, narrative, quests, combat,
jobs, enemies, bosses, locations, equipment, and progression. Ask for human
direction only where the brief leaves a consequential product choice genuinely
ambiguous; otherwise make a clearly-labelled, original design proposal.

This is a discovery step, not a paraphrase. Infer the product disciplines and
system families implied by the brief and create
`specification/00-domain-discovery.md`. Separate explicit requests, necessary
implied capabilities, safe assumptions, and decisions needing human approval.
For example, a role-playing game brief may imply narrative, world, characters,
quests, dialogue, combat, progression, content tools, and balancing; an IDE may
imply language services, editing, debugging, extensions, workspace/indexing,
and source control; an operating system may imply kernels, hardware support,
security, installers, system settings, and developer tools. These are examples,
not templates. Never apply a fixed taxonomy to every product.

## Acceptance criteria

- The brief separates requested behaviour, assumptions, non-goals, and unresolved decisions.
- `01-creative-direction.md` exists before feature, technical, visual, or
  audio planning begins. It establishes original narrative/content direction
  where relevant and explicitly records when narrative, visual, or audio scope
  is not material to the product.
- Every game has an original narrative/gameplay framing, visual direction, and
  audio direction from discovery; these are proportionate to the game but are
  never deferred as optional polish.
- Every game has `01-game-identity.md` with stable `GAME-*` design-unit IDs
  before genre-specific mechanics or content are planned.
- Every game has an evidence-based game-design-signals classification and a
  candidate-module decision record. It states both selected and rejected
  systems, so later stages cannot accidentally use an RPG checklist for trivia,
  a builder checklist for a puzzle, or any other fixed genre template.
- Domain discovery identifies the complete candidate system map appropriate to
  this product category, including content, operations, tooling, and quality
  disciplines—not only screens explicitly named by the user.
- Every inferred system is labelled explicit, implied, assumed, deferred, or
  requires-decision, with rationale and a source reference.
- Every selected target system has an explicit first-release role, input model, delivery constraint, and out-of-scope boundary.
- The named project workspace from the supplied initial brief exists under `projects/` before later contracts create its documents.
- `projects/<project-slug>/INITIAL.md` is preserved as the source of truth; unknowns are recorded rather than guessed.
- A reference-boundary table prohibits copied branding, copy, assets, layouts, and interaction choreography.
- First-release scope and later-roadmap scope are explicit and measurable.
