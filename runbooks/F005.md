## Objective

Create `projects/<project-slug>/planning/05-delivery-map.md`, an ordered inventory of
small, independently reviewable implementation tasks derived from F001–F004
and the target repository's actual structure. Partition shared foundations from
target-specific adapters, UI, input, distribution, and certification work.

Treat creative production as a dependency graph, not polish after coding.
Where applicable, include narrative/content packages and their validation,
original visual assets and integration, music/sound generation and integration,
accessibility equivalents, provenance review, and QA. Place these work items
early enough that gameplay, UI, asset loading, audio triggers, saves, and tests
are designed around the real content rather than placeholder-only assumptions.

For a game, create `planning/game-production-inventory.md` from the game-design
bible. It lists every selected `GAME-*` unit and, where relevant, its design
cards (characters, units, enemies, bosses, locations, quests, moves/abilities,
items, buildings, levels, UI surfaces, visual asset families, and audio cues),
with scope/quantity, dependency, original-design brief, owner, and verification
route. It is the bridge between design intent and bounded implementation work.
Create the matching machine-readable
`planning/game-production-inventory.json`. Each entry identifies its design
unit, selected module, content/design-card type, finite item or catalogue
batch, planned quantity, original brief, dependencies, asset/audio IDs,
implementation outcome, owner, and verification. Every selected module and
every `GAME-*` unit must have at least one inventory entry. A finite RPG roster
therefore names and describes its actual first-release party, bosses, towns,
quests, or items as appropriate; a football-trivia catalogue instead has
explicit category/difficulty/type quotas and small editorial batches with
source validation. Do not use vague entries such as “make game content.”

## Acceptance criteria

- Each task has a bounded outcome, dependency set, verification strategy, and file/data ownership.
- Foundation, domain, features, accessibility, trust, operations, documentation, QA, and release work are included when in scope.
- Narrative/content, visual assets, audio, provenance, integration, and their
  accessibility/QA work are included as owned first-class tasks whenever the
  creative direction declares them in scope.
- Every game-design unit has a production-inventory entry and is either planned
  for the first release, explicitly deferred, or rejected with a reason.
- The JSON inventory proves every selected game module has concrete production
  work. It distinguishes finite, named creative content from scalable
  catalogues, and gives both a bounded, independently verifiable production
  plan.
- Browser-impacting tasks own unique future Playwright specification paths.
